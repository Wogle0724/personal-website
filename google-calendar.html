Google · Calendar Sync

June 2026

Problem

Most people don't have one life — they have several, split across several Google accounts. A student inbox, a personal Gmail, a professional address. Each comes with its own calendar and its own tasks, and there's no honest way to see them together.

So you improvise. Schedule a Zoom call from your professional account and you invite your personal account too — just so the event shows up where you actually look. You forward task lists to yourself. You keep a mental map of "which calendar has the thing." It mostly works, until the one time it doesn't:

  • Invisible conflicts. Someone asks for a 2:00 call on your work calendar. You say yes — because the lunch you booked on your personal calendar three weeks ago isn't in front of you. The double-book only surfaces when you're already late.
  • Manual cross-posting. Keeping an event visible everywhere means inviting yourself, copying details by hand, and cleaning up duplicates later.
  • No single source of truth. To answer "am I free Thursday?" you check two or three calendars in two or three tabs — and even then, you are the one who has to notice the clash.

The core issue: your time is one thing, but your calendars treat it as many — and the job of reconciling them falls entirely on you.

Proposed Solution — Master Schedule

Master Schedule is two halves of one idea.

  • See everything in one view. Connect all of your Google accounts and view their calendars in parallel, in one place. Each account gets a friendly name with its email in parentheses in the left sidebar — School (o.wyatt@wustl.edu), Personal, Work — each with its own toggle. Check the ones you want; the Master Schedule overlays them in a single grid.
  • Every event keeps its identity. Events from different accounts appear side by side in the same time slots, each carrying a colored stripe that ties it back to the account it lives on. One glance tells you not just when you're busy, but which life is busy — without opening a single extra tab.
  • An AI agent that already knows. Seeing everything only helps if you remember to look — so an agent watches all of your calendars at once, across every connected account, and speaks up before a mistake happens. You decide how much it sees; the more you grant, the more it can protect.
  • It reaches across accounts that can't see each other. A conflict hiding on your personal calendar gets caught while you're replying from your work one — because the agent isn't trapped inside a single account the way each calendar is.

The moment it earns its place

You get a networking message: "Could you do a quick call at 2:00 today?" You start typing "Yeah, that sounds great" — and the AI agent cuts in: "Heads up — you have Lunch with Jordanna at 2:00 on your Personal calendar."

You flip on your Personal calendar in the sidebar to check. There it is, in green: Lunch with Jordanna, 2:00. A conflict you'd have agreed to blind. You delete the reply, send "Can we do 1:00 instead?", and get back "Yes, sounds good." Crisis averted before it existed — because the agent was watching the calendar you weren't.

Master Schedule doesn't ask you to merge your identities — it respects that they're separate on purpose. It just stops making you the integration layer. The result: your calendar finally knows everything you know — and an agent that never looks away makes sure you act on it.
